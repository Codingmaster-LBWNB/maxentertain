# SEO & Online Exposure

The premise: a direct-booking site that only ranks for its own brand name is a brochure. The job of SEO is to capture guests *before* they go to Airbnb.

## The search intent hierarchy for STRs

People searching for holiday rentals typically move through three stages. Rank for the top two and you own demand.

| Stage | Example query | Volume | Intent | Typical result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inspiration | "mornington peninsula family holiday" | High | Researching region | Blog / listicle / guide |
| Consideration | "6 bedroom holiday house mornington peninsula" | Medium | Comparing properties | Airbnb / Stayz / booking aggregators |
| Decision | "max entertain beachside retreat" | Low (brand) | Ready to book | Your site |

Most STR sites only rank for stage 3. The leverage is in stages 1 and 2.

## The 5-page minimum for an STR direct-book site

One-page sites cap your organic ceiling. Every STR should have at least:

1. **Homepage** — brand + primary CTA + hero story
2. **Property detail page** — amenities, sleeping config, house rules (this is what the current homepage largely is)
3. **Local area guide** — "Things to do on the Mornington Peninsula" with real opinions, not a Wikipedia copy
4. **Segment landing page(s)** — "Family holidays", "Corporate retreats", "Milestone birthdays" — each ranks for its own query cluster
5. **Blog / journal** — "Best beaches near Tootgarook", "What to do on the Peninsula in winter", "Peninsula golf courses ranked", "Mornington Peninsula with kids in the rain" — topical authority stacks

For maxentertain.com: currently a one-page site. The local-area section is a start but isn't indexable as separate content.

## The technical SEO baseline

Run through this list on any STR site. For maxentertain.com, check each:

- [ ] **Title tags** unique per page, 55–60 chars, include location + property type + key differentiator
- [ ] **Meta descriptions** 150–160 chars, include a benefit and a soft CTA
- [ ] **Schema.org markup**: `LodgingBusiness` + `Product` + `AggregateRating` + `Review` — huge for rich snippets
- [ ] **Open Graph tags** for every page (Facebook/WhatsApp preview)
- [ ] **Sitemap.xml** and submitted to Google Search Console + Bing Webmaster Tools
- [ ] **Image alt text** with descriptive local-keyword language ("Solar-heated pool at beachfront retreat in Tootgarook" not "pool.jpg")
- [ ] **Core Web Vitals** all green on mobile (LCP <2.5s, CLS <0.1, INP <200ms)
- [ ] **Canonical tags** on every page to prevent OTA duplicate-content penalties
- [ ] **hreflang** if targeting international guests (en-AU vs en-US matters less than en vs zh)
- [ ] **Internal linking** — every blog post links to the property page with varied anchor text
- [ ] **HTTPS everywhere, HTTP → HTTPS redirect, www → apex (or vice versa) 301** consistent

## Google Business Profile (often skipped, high-impact)

STRs debate whether they "count" as a business Google will list. The answer for a named property like MAX Entertain Beachside Retreat is yes — it's a lodging. Benefits:

- Ranks in local pack for "holiday rental near me" / "holiday house Tootgarook"
- Enables photo + review display in search results
- Adds a Maps pin that drives in-person awareness

Set up: create a GBP with category "Holiday Apartment Rental" or "Vacation Home Rental Agency". Verify by postcard (takes 1–2 weeks). Cross-post photos from the site. Ask happy guests to leave a Google review in addition to an Airbnb one — Google reviews are platform-independent and move direct-book organic significantly.

## OTA SEO (the other half of exposure)

Direct-book SEO is only half the game. Airbnb and Booking.com have internal ranking algorithms that are effectively the same game — rank for the same intent, in their ecosystem.

Key levers on Airbnb (ordered by impact):
1. **Response rate & response time** — both must be near-100% / sub-hour. Automations matter here.
2. **Instant Book enabled** (with smart filters for safety if party risk is a concern)
3. **Review count + rating** — 4.8+ is table stakes for luxury. Drive review velocity.
4. **Listing completeness** — every field filled, 20+ photos, full descriptions. Airbnb literally has a completeness score.
5. **Price competitiveness** — Airbnb's algorithm weights price against cohort. Dynamic pricing tools help.
6. **Booking conversion** — the % of people who view and then book. Tighten hero photo and first 3 photos.
7. **New-listing boost** — expires after ~90 days. If JR ever re-lists, exploit this window aggressively.

Key levers on Booking.com:
1. **Preferred Partner program** (higher commission, higher rank)
2. **Genius program** (discount for Genius members, boosts visibility)
3. **Review score** — weighted even more than Airbnb
4. **Content score** — Booking shows you a completeness % in the extranet; get to 100%

Avoid: offering the same minimum pricing on your direct site as on OTAs. Airbnb's rate parity soft-enforcement will drop your ranking. Offer a "direct-book perk" (bottle of wine, late checkout, pool-heating upgrade) rather than a headline price cut, so the OTA price stays competitive.

## Content strategy (the 90-day plan)

A blog only works if it has a topical point of view. Generic "top 10 things to do" posts get nowhere. Pattern for high-ROI content:

**Week 1–4:** Publish 4 anchor posts, each 1500–2000 words:
1. "The Mornington Peninsula with three generations: how to keep everyone happy"
2. "A golf weekend on the Peninsula: courses, logistics, and where to stay"
3. "Peninsula Hot Springs vs Alba Thermal Springs: which one, when, and why"
4. "Mornington Peninsula in winter: why it's our favourite season"

Each post links to the property page with segment-appropriate anchor text ("our family-sized beach house", "a 6-bedroom base for golf groups", etc.)

**Week 5–12:** Publish weekly supporting posts (500–800 words) targeting long-tail queries. Interlink generously. Add fresh photos from guests (with permission).

**Distribution:** post each one to a Facebook group for Peninsula travel, share to a small Instagram account, email to past guests. Don't expect organic to move for 3–6 months — this is a compounding asset.

## Social media (be honest about ROI)

For a 6BR luxury STR:
- **Instagram** is worth maintaining at low effort. 1 post/week, 3 stories. The goal is social proof for guests who find you elsewhere and check the handle, not discovery.
- **TikTok** is a distraction unless JR enjoys it. The audience is wrong (younger, lower spend, more price-sensitive) and the conversion path is poor.
- **Facebook** matters for Peninsula community groups ("Mornington Peninsula Holidays" etc.) where local recommendations spread. Be a helpful member, not a spammer.
- **Pinterest** is underrated for STRs — evergreen content, visual, the audience plans holidays 3–6 months out. Worth 1 hour/week.
- **LinkedIn** matters only for the service business (see scaling reference), not for guest acquisition.

## Measurement

Install Google Analytics 4 + Google Search Console + a privacy-friendly session recorder (Microsoft Clarity is free and quiet). Track:

- Organic traffic by landing page (are the content plays working?)
- Direct-book conversion rate from the homepage (baseline this before optimizing)
- Inquiry → booking rate (this is the critical metric)
- Revenue per visitor (the ultimate number)

Review monthly. Any experiment that doesn't move one of these four in 90 days gets killed.

## The link-building moves that actually work for STRs

Ignore generic "guest post" advice. These work:

1. **Get listed in real local guides** — Visit Mornington Peninsula, Visit Victoria, local tourism boards. Requires outreach, not SEO magic.
2. **Partner with local businesses** — wineries, hot springs, golf clubs. Offer a "stay-and-play" package with reciprocal linking.
3. **Wedding / event venue directories** — even if you don't host weddings, accommodation-for-wedding-parties is a listed category on most directories.
4. **Corporate retreat directories** — underserved category, the property qualifies.
5. **Local news / lifestyle press** — pitch a story ("the family-owned Peninsula retreat winning Airbnb awards") to The Age, Broadsheet, Concrete Playground. One placement = a decade of referral traffic.

Ignore: paid link services, PBNs, directory submission mills. Google's algorithm is mature enough that low-quality links are worse than no links.
