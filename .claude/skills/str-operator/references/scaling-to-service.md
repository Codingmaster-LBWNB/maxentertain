# Scaling to a Service

The path from "I run one property well" to "I run a service others pay for" is the interesting and dangerous part. Most hosts who try this end up as under-priced VAs. Here's how not to.

## The honest starting truth

JR's advantage:
- Tech stack already built (N8N, MongoDB, Next.js, Gemini AI integration)
- A lighthouse case study (award-winning 6BR, 120+ 5-star reviews, direct-book site)
- CS background — can build what other hosts can't
- Melbourne / Peninsula local network to start with

JR's disadvantage:
- Solo
- Still a full-time student until end of 2026
- No operational history servicing *other people's* properties (very different from running your own)

The service business must fit inside these constraints. That means: high-margin, low-headcount, tech-enabled. Not traditional property management.

## What you're actually selling (pick one, resist pick-three)

The three plausible wedges, each very different:

### A. **Done-for-you direct-booking site**
- One-time build: $3,500–$8,000 AUD
- Monthly hosting + maintenance: $150–$400
- Includes: site on the maxentertain.com stack, payment integration, OTA iCal sync, basic automations
- TAM: every host who relies on Airbnb/Booking and wants their own brand
- Risk: commoditized — Lodgify, Hostaway, iGMS offer similar at scale

### B. **Automation-as-a-service for hosts with a channel manager**
- Setup fee: $1,500–$3,000 per property
- Monthly: $100–$300 per property
- Includes: N8N workflows for inquiry routing, pre-stay drip, review automation, AI message drafting
- TAM: hosts who already use Hospitable/Hostaway but want the glue layer
- Risk: low because most hosts don't know what automation can do — high because it requires handholding

### C. **Full property-tech stack (A + B + SEO + content)**
- Onboarding: $5,000–$15,000
- Monthly: $400–$1,000 per property
- Includes: everything above plus blog writing, OTA listing optimization, pricing tool setup, review management
- TAM: boutique multi-property owners (2–10 properties) who've outgrown DIY but aren't big enough for Guesty
- Risk: operational load is high; this is where solo operators break

**Recommended first offer: B, because** setup is technical (your edge), monthly is recurring (revenue stability), and churn is low (once workflows are in, they stay in). A is a foot-in-the-door product. C is year-2+, after you know what clients actually need.

## The ICP (ideal client profile)

Not all hosts are good clients. Rank your targets:

| ICP | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2–5 property boutique host, tech-curious, frustrated with Airbnb commission | 🟢 High | Money + motivation + appreciation for tech |
| Owner of 1 premium property ($500+/night) in Mornington/Daylesford/Sorrento | 🟢 High | Can afford you, wants direct bookings, easy case study |
| Single Airbnb host with $200/night property | 🔴 Low | Can't afford you, will haggle, high-touch, low-margin |
| 10+ property manager who already has a PMS | 🟡 Medium | Has budget, but has vendors — you're a "nice-to-have" add-on |
| Off-grid / unique property with storytelling | 🟢 High | Content moat + willing to pay for marketing |
| Airbnb arbitrage operators | 🔴 Low | Thin margins, ethically murky, high turnover |

Target ICPs #1, #2, #5. Ignore the others until you're 10 clients in.

## Pricing philosophy

Three principles:

1. **Never price by hours.** Price by value. "I'll save you 10 hours/week and lift revenue 15%" justifies $500/month. "It takes me 5 hours to set up" justifies $500 once. Same work, 10x difference.
2. **Setup fee matters more than monthly for solo.** Cash up front funds the business and filters tire-kickers. $2,500 setup + $150/mo >> $0 setup + $300/mo for your cashflow.
3. **Raise prices on new clients every 5 sign-ups.** Existing clients grandfathered. You will under-price the first clients; that's fine if you plan for it.

A specific starting price ladder for the automation-as-a-service offer:

- **Clients 1–3 (design partners):** $1,000 setup + $100/mo, 6-month minimum, heavy input requested in exchange
- **Clients 4–10:** $1,800 setup + $175/mo
- **Clients 11–25:** $2,500 setup + $250/mo
- **Clients 26+:** $3,500 setup + $350/mo, consider a tier above with SLA for full-service clients

## The first client is a case study, not a sale

Your first paying client should be someone you know (Jason has a network; Peninsula hosts know each other). Offer:
- 60% off the real price
- In exchange for: full before/after access, a testimonial, a referral to 3 other hosts, and permission to use their data (anonymized) in marketing

The revenue from the first client is nearly irrelevant. The case study is what sells clients 2–10.

## The GTM sequence for clients 1–10

Do not build a website, do not run ads, do not do content marketing first. The sequence is:

1. **Month 1–2:** build out the MVP service using maxentertain.com as the reference implementation. Document everything.
2. **Month 3:** close design partner #1 through direct outreach (DM Peninsula hosts on Airbnb, message them via their site, LinkedIn connect). Aim for 1 yes out of 30 asks.
3. **Month 4:** deliver design partner 1, collect data. Close design partner 2 while delivering 1.
4. **Month 5–6:** close partners 3–5 through referrals + one case study asset.
5. **Month 7:** start writing publicly (Twitter/LinkedIn, one post a week). Topics: "what I automated for a 6BR Peninsula property this week", "why your OTA response time is secretly tanking your revenue".
6. **Month 8+:** inbound starts trickling. Close month 6+ clients at higher prices.

**Do not scale via paid ads in year 1.** The unit economics require 3+ clients of validation first, and paid ads on a $300/mo subscription take 4–6 months to pay back.

## Productization traps to avoid

- **The bespoke trap.** Every client wants one custom thing. Say no or charge $500 for it. One custom workflow per client destroys margin.
- **The SLA trap.** Don't promise 24/7 response in year 1. You're one person. Set expectations at "business hours, 12-hour response, 4-hour on critical" and charge extra for anything tighter.
- **The feature-factory trap.** Clients will ask for more features. Ship a roadmap, not a to-do list. One major release per quarter.
- **The under-priced-VA trap.** If you're doing $30/hr work, you're a VA, not a tech founder. Track every client against a 70%+ margin target; fire the ones below.
- **The "it works on mine" trap.** maxentertain.com is one stack. Clients will have Wix, WordPress, custom PHP, a cousin-built site. Decide: do you support their stack (services) or move them to yours (productized)? The latter scales, the former doesn't.

## The boring but critical legal / ops layer

Solo doesn't mean casual. Before the first paid client:

- **Business structure:** Australian PTY LTD or sole trader with proper insurance. Ask an accountant — don't freestyle.
- **Professional indemnity insurance:** handling bookings for others is a liability. ~$1,000/year.
- **Service agreement template:** scope, deliverables, SLA, termination, data handling, GST. Get a lawyer to review the first one ($500–$1,000 one-time).
- **Data processing agreement (DPA):** you'll have access to guest PII. Covered by privacy law. This must be in writing.
- **Separate bank account, separate Stripe:** do not commingle maxentertain.com cash with service revenue. Makes tax + any future acquisition cleaner.

## What the product actually *is* (the operational reality)

Clients don't buy "N8N workflows". They buy outcomes. Frame the offer as:

> "I set up the same tech stack that took MAX Entertain from 120 to 200+ reviews and 40% direct-booking rate, tuned to your property. One setup fee, one monthly. I maintain it. You get the dashboard."

The deliverable list per client (automation-as-a-service):
- Direct-booking site setup or integration (if they have one)
- Inquiry → CRM + auto-reply workflow
- OTA message unification (via Hospitable or similar)
- Pre-stay guest drip (4 templates customized to their property)
- Review request automation
- Monthly revenue dashboard (Notion/Google Sheets or a small Next.js dashboard for premium tier)
- 1 hour/month strategy call

Onboarding template, once built, should take 8–12 hours per new client. Monthly maintenance should be <2 hours per client. That's the margin target.

## Exit / scale options (thinking ahead)

At 10–20 clients, you're at ~$5k–$8k MRR and you've hit your ceiling as a solo. Options:
- **Hire:** one Virtual Assistant in a low-cost region to handle setup + L1 support. Margin drops but time freed.
- **Productize further:** turn the service into a self-serve SaaS. Lodgify and similar prove the market exists. You'd be selling the tech, not the service. Higher ceiling, much harder product.
- **Sell:** small agencies with recurring revenue and <$200k ARR are acquirable by property-tech companies looking for a bookings channel.
- **Stay solo:** at $8k MRR, if margins are 80%, that's $6.4k/mo profit with 30–40 hr/week. Not a bad life, no investors to please.

Don't optimize for the exit in year 1. Optimize for a repeatable first 5 clients. Everything else is speculation.

## The 30-day "are we serious about this" test

Before investing months, spend 30 days doing this:
- Pick 10 target hosts in the Peninsula / Daylesford / Sorrento region
- DM each one: "I run a similar property [link maxentertain.com]. I've built some tech that lifted our direct bookings significantly. Happy to show you in a 15-min call, no sales pitch."
- Measure: how many reply? How many book a call? What do they actually ask for?

If fewer than 3 reply, the offer or positioning is wrong — iterate before building more. If 3+ reply and 1+ books, you have a signal to invest another 3 months.
